ScrollCatcher Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026

ScrollCatcher estimates how many Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels you watch and measures time spent in your selected apps. These features process data on your phone. No account is required.

Data access and local processing

ScrollCatcher accesses app-usage information and, if you enable its optional accessibility service, limited information about the app and screen layout currently in use. This information is used only for the features explained below.

The app does not transmit this information to the developer or third parties. It has no internet permission, advertising, developer analytics or remote crash reporting. Local charts and totals are provided for you; they are not sent to us.

What stays on your phone

  • Daily records: dates, selected app identifiers, time spent in each selected app and estimated short-video counts.
  • Your settings: tracked apps, tracking preferences, daily target, theme, live-counter appearance and position, and your accessibility consent choice.

These records are stored in the app's private storage. ScrollCatcher is configured to exclude its data from Android cloud backup and device transfer. It does not upload a backup to a server.

Usage Access

If you grant Usage Access in Android settings, ScrollCatcher reads app-activity events and their times to calculate how long your selected apps were in the foreground. Events from other apps and device-activity events, such as the screen turning off, help determine when a selected app stopped being used.

The app saves daily time totals for selected apps, rather than a raw log of those events. Usage Access is not used to read messages, passwords or screen contents. App-usage information is processed only on your phone and is not shared.

You can decline or revoke Usage Access in Android Settings → Apps → Special app access → Usage access → ScrollCatcher. Menu names may differ by device. Without this permission, the app-time feature is unavailable.

Accessibility Service

The optional swipe counter uses Android's Accessibility Service. ScrollCatcher is not an accessibility tool designed primarily for people with disabilities. A separate in-app disclosure explains the service before you are asked to enable it. The service requires both your in-app agreement and Android accessibility permission before it processes events.

While tracking is on, the service:

  • Detects which app is on screen, including other apps, to show or hide the optional live counter and recognize when an app session ends. Temporary system or keyboard windows are distinguished from leaving an app.
  • Uses screen layout, view identifiers, temporary node identities, bounds and scroll positions within selected supported apps to estimate when a new video is shown. For YouTube, it recognizes when a Shorts player page has settled.

The service does not read on-screen text, messages or passwords, take screenshots, record video or audio, or perform taps or swipes for you. It does not save video content or a list of the videos you watch. Temporary layout and node information is processed in memory; only daily counts per app are saved. No accessibility data is transmitted or shared.

You can choose No thanks without enabling the counter and still use app-time tracking if you grant Usage Access. You can withdraw accessibility consent in ScrollCatcher's settings or disable the service in Android Settings → Accessibility → ScrollCatcher swipe counter. The service remains blocked after clearing local app data until you agree again.

Hide for this session hides the live counter but does not stop counting. Use the app's Tracking switch or revoke accessibility access to stop that processing.

Sharing, advertising and accounts

ScrollCatcher does not sell or share your app-usage records, accessibility information or settings. It has no accounts, advertising SDKs or developer analytics SDKs. There is no server-side account or usage database associated with the app.

Retention and deleting your data

Daily records and settings remain in the app's private storage until you clear them or uninstall the app; they are not automatically deleted after the seven-day chart period.

Tap Clear all local data in ScrollCatcher's settings to delete its saved records and reset its preferences, including in-app accessibility consent. Uninstalling the app removes its private app data. Revoking a permission stops access through that permission but does not itself delete previously saved records.

Clearing ScrollCatcher's data does not erase usage history maintained separately by Android or revoke Android permissions. If tracking and Usage Access remain enabled, time totals may be recalculated from Android's existing history. To prevent this, revoke Usage Access in Android settings. Previously cleared swipe counts cannot be reconstructed from that history.

External pages and contact

This policy is hosted on GitHub Pages and may be opened in your browser. Requests made by that browser or website are separate from the app's offline data processing. If you contact the developer by email, your email provider transmits the information you choose to include. Please do not send passwords or private app-usage records.

Children

ScrollCatcher is not intended for children under 13.

Changes

If the app's data practices change, this policy will be updated with a new date. Any additional consent required for changed data access will be requested within the app.
